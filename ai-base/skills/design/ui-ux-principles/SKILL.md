# Universal Design Principles

A cross-disciplinary catalog of design principles (based on *Universal
Principles of Design*) applied to UI/UX work: reviews, feedback, documentation,
and decisions. The full 100-principle catalog with definitions and cross-links
lives in `references/principles.md` — consult it rather than relying on memory.

## When to use

- Reviewing/critiquing a UI, mockup, wireframe, or screenshot.
- Writing a design doc or justifying design choices.
- Weighing tradeoffs (flexibility vs usability, aesthetics vs function).
- Explaining a named principle or pattern.
- Trigger phrases: "review this UI", "design feedback", "Fitts' Law",
  "Hick's Law", "affordance", "visual hierarchy", "progressive disclosure",
  "cognitive load", "Gestalt", "design tradeoff".

## Instructions

1. Read `references/principles.md` when you need the authoritative catalog.
2. For a review: lead with strengths, then issues, then concrete improvements.
3. Cite only 3–7 principles per review — the most relevant, highest impact.
4. Organize feedback by impact (high → low), not alphabetically.
5. Acknowledge when principles conflict and recommend based on context.
6. Keep it conversational; avoid "principle dumping."

### Three modes

- **Review & feedback** — opportunities and issues through the lens of relevant principles.
- **Documentation** — ground recommendations in principles and preempt objections.
- **Concept explanation** — define, make concrete for UI, connect 2–3 related principles.

### Five organizing questions

| Question | Example principles |
|---|---|
| How do I influence perception? | Affordance, Alignment, Figure-Ground, Proximity, Similarity, Signal-to-Noise |
| How do I help people learn? | Chunking, Progressive Disclosure, Legibility, Recognition over Recall, Serial Position |
| How do I enhance usability? | Fitts' Law, Hick's Law, Consistency, Forgiveness, Constraint, Mapping |
| How do I increase appeal? | Aesthetic-Usability Effect, Color, Rule of Thirds, Symmetry, Storytelling |
| How do I make better decisions? | Cost-Benefit, Iteration, Ockham's Razor, Prototyping, Weakest Link |

### Prioritize for UI/UX

- **Interaction**: Affordance, Constraint, Consistency, Fitts' Law, Hick's Law, Forgiveness, Progressive Disclosure, Recognition over Recall, Feedback Loop.
- **Visual/layout**: Alignment, Hierarchy, Figure-Ground, Proximity, Similarity, Signal-to-Noise, Color, Rule of Thirds.
- **Information**: Chunking, Inverted Pyramid, Layering, Readability, Legibility, Picture Superiority.
- **Strategic**: 80/20, Aesthetic-Usability, Cost-Benefit, Flexibility-Usability Tradeoff, Ockham's Razor, Accessibility.

## Examples

### Good — tied to a principle and actionable

> The nav has 12 top-level items, which fights **Hick's Law** (more choices =
> slower decisions). Group into 4–5 categories with progressive disclosure.

### Bad — principle dumping, no action

> **Hick's Law**: too many items. **Progressive Disclosure**: hide some.
> **Chunking**: group them.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it hurts | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Applying a principle by name without context | Reads as jargon, not help | Explain the tradeoff concretely |
| Listing all 100 principles | Overwhelms, dilutes signal | Cite 3–7 relevant ones |
| Ignoring conflicting principles | One-sided advice | Name the tension, pick by context |
| Presenting opinion as principle | Not defensible | Ground each claim in a principle |
